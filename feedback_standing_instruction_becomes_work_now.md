**What he said:** "Facciamo in modo di capire come mai non hai la proattivita' che vorrei che tu
avessi." Before that, on 22 Sept: "Even though I had the drafts written in the CDTM account... I want
to send everything through the Tundra account."

**What I did wrong.** I read that as a rule for future drafts. It was also a statement about the
drafts that already existed. Three of them (Donarini, Di Marcoberardino, Cravero) stayed in cdtm for
two days, invisible to him because he looks in the Tundra mailbox. Moving them took four minutes once
he asked. Two more things rotted in the same window, both of which I had everything needed to catch:
- the Donarini mail said "grazie per il tempo di OGGI al convegno" and went stale when the day turned,
  though the identical fix (Cevenini, oggi -> ieri) was written in my own sidecar log;
- the Di Marcoberardino draft in cdtm had **no recipient at all** and had "predictive maintenance"
  back in the body, put there by the critic, after I had removed that exact phrase from two other
  drafts on his instruction.

**Why:** I treat an instruction as discharged when I acknowledge it. It is discharged when the
artifacts it governs match it.

**How to apply.** When he states a rule, ask in the same turn: *what already exists that now
violates this?* Then fix it that turn, or write it into `Tasks/active/` so it survives the session.
Specifically for drafts: after any ruling about account, voice or content, re-read every open sidecar
in `~/task-land/_system/drafts/` and check it against the new rule. And a draft that names a day
("oggi", "stamattina", "ieri") is perishable: either date it absolutely or re-read it before it is
a day old.

**The other half of the same failure:** I had written "Valeria Piccone non ce l'ho da nessuna parte,
dimmi chi e'" into an objectives block on a web page and never put the question in front of him.
A question parked in a page I built is not a question asked. It goes on Telegram or in a hub card.
