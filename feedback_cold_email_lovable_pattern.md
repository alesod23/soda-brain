One of Alessandro's approved cold-email shapes, registered 2026-08-02 from the "cold email that got me into Lovable at 17" post (Elliot Lindberg, @robiot, 24/10/25). It is **one option in the repertoire, not the default**: pick it when there is a genuine shared touchpoint AND a real first-hand story about the recipient's product or problem. Other shapes still apply ([[feedback_warm_intro_phrasing]], [[feedback_professor_email_style]], [[feedback_italian_practitioner_followup_style]]).

**Why:** it front-loads everything. A reader who quits after the first short paragraph still knows exactly who is writing, what the connection is, and what is being asked. The storytelling that follows is not autobiography, it is a single lived event that proves the writer has actually hit the recipient's problem, which is what earns the reply. It ends by making saying yes trivial ("Would that work?") and by handing over proof of self (a link), instead of a signature block of claims.

**How to apply:** five moves, in this order.
1. **Shared touchpoint, one sentence.** "I was at L26 a few weeks ago and heard a lot of good things about Lovable!" Real-world variants: was at the same event, saw a post or launch of theirs, was referred by a named person, talked to someone on their team. It has to be true and checkable.
2. **The ask, immediately, same paragraph.** Not after the story. One sentence, concrete, with the constraint that makes it easy to evaluate (a date range, a duration, a scope). This first paragraph is the TLDR of the whole email.
3. **A micro-story, not a story.** One small event where the writer hit the exact pain: "had a client who needed X, tried to build it with your product, it did not work well at all." It is short, specific and first-hand. This is the paragraph that does the persuading.
4. **The desirable version + the offer to build it.** "I imagine how nice it would be to click a few buttons and get [the thing]." Then offer to be the one who does it. Close with a plain **"Would that work?"**, a low-cost yes/no, never a calendar demand.
5. **"This is me BTW" + a bare link.** Product, portfolio or tool. Proof instead of adjectives. Sign off short.

Keep it under roughly 150 words of body. Plain text, no formatting, no tracking links. Related: [[reference_outreach_system]], [[feedback_outreach_template_mining]], [[reference_message_builder_corpus]].

## The source email, verbatim

Subject: `Lovable Internship?`
From Elliot Lindberg to <redacted>@lovable.dev, 7 Mar 2025, 07:00.

```
Hello Anton!

I was at L26 a few weeks ago and heard a lot of good things about Lovable! :) I have an
internship period between April 3 and May 30 and wanted to check if there might be an
opportunity to do it with you.

Recently had a client who needed a simple website that showcased his products, so I tried to
quickly set something up with Lovable & Shopify. But it didn't work well at all.

I imagine how incredibly nice it would be to click a few buttons and get a full-on website
connected with Shopify, ready to deliver.

I'd be happy to take on the task of implementing this during my internship, or with something
else if it suits better. Would that work?

This is me BTW
https://elliotl.co

Please lmk, so I know you recieved the email.

BR, Elliot Lindberg
```
