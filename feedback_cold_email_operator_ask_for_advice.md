Set 2026-08-10 after he rewrote my Nehal Kapadia (MGH biomedical engineering) draft and said *"your email was terrible."* **Treat my version as the anti-pattern.** Confirmed line by line via his own edits plus a preference pass.

**Why:** to a cold operator he has to convince, every sentence that sounds like selling costs him the reply. The whole email is an *ask to learn*, not a pitch. His edits all pull in that one direction.

## Never write these (all mine, all cut)
- **"I am not trying to sell you anything."** His words: *"i hate saying 'not trying to sell anything' -> who tf says that? its so suspicious."* A denial plants the idea.
- **"Forgive the unusual route:"** Self-deprecating apology for cold-emailing. Do not pre-apologise for existing.
- **"where this actually breaks in a hospital your size."** *"too salesy, assumes."* Never presume their problems back at them. Keep it on the **learning** side, e.g. "how it works".
- **Traction stats** ("spoken with over 100 clinical engineers"). *"the only real traction we need to show is how i put it"* = **"We are negotiating our first pilots in Germany and Italy."** That phrasing is the preferred one for now.
- **Feature lists** ("natural-language querying, service and contract decisions, repair-versus-replace history"). *"too long, prefer to keep it short also to not give out everything."* One plain phrase: **"predictive maintenance"**.

## Register depends on the audience
| Audience | How he frames himself | How much he reveals |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct responsible / operator** (Kapadia) | **student-founder** — *convey it, don't necessarily say it*; he has to convince, and "co-founder" reads salesy. "She won't remember the company name." | Least. Short, hold detail back. |
| **Someone giving intros** | co-founder — *"they know whatsup"* | More |
| **Investor** | co-founder | Most, and **open with the vision**: *"intelligence layer for medical equipment"* or *"building brains for hospitals starting w medic eq"* |

## Structure he chose
1. How he got the contact (the referral does the credibility work). No ask attached here — *"already clear i wanted to reach her from 1st paragraph."*
2. Who he is + what they build, small and plain: "a recent graduate of [full university name]. With a friend (an AI engineer), we're trying to build..." Then the one traction line + "we'd love to learn more about the US market."
3. **The ask, last, in its own paragraph**, with the flattery attached: "Your expertise would help us better understand the field."
4. Availability, then a full signature (title, phone, company).

**University: use the SHORTEST name the recipient will recognise** (refined 2026-09-02). Never "CDTM" outside Munich, nobody knows it. But the long "Technical University of Munich" is only for people who would NOT recognise the short form (a US hospital operator, a French institution). **Anyone working in Germany, or in tech, knows TUM: write "a TUM" / "at TUM".** Spelling it out to them is noise. See [[feedback_outreach_ask_first_peer_voice]].

"I" vs "we" is *"not a big deal"* — don't spend edits there.

Subject line that passed (2026-08-10): **`EU student-founder, referred by your team: advice?`** — carries EU + student-founder + the referral + an advice ask, in 50 chars. He prefers "student-founder from EU" phrasing over "European student founder".

**2026-09-08 RULING - ask position DEFAULT is now FIRST, everywhere.** During the email-review-contract pass he resolved the conflict between this file's "the ask, last, in its own paragraph" and [[feedback_outreach_ask_first_peer_voice]]'s "the ask goes FIRST": *"by default, we say ask first. We use top-down communication, and it can be overruled if I may prompt or structure, I specifically put the ask at a later point."* So the structure above (#3, ask last) is NOT the default for cold operator emails any more; it applies only when he explicitly structures a mail that way.
