When a message references something learned from an organisation's own site, write it as something
you know, not as something you looked up.

- Good: "I understand it's CMIA guidance to ..."
- Bad: "The website says it's CMIA guidance to ..."

His words, 2026-09-10: *"I understand it's CMIA guidance to — better than 'The website... says'
since the latter seems AI."*

**Why:** citing the page gives away that the sentence was assembled from a scrape. A human who
read the same page would just know the thing, and would say so. Naming the source is the tell.

**How to apply:** applies to every outreach channel and language. The existing hedging rule still
holds and does not conflict: hedge the claim about *the person* ("I saw you co-chair...", "ho visto
che...", "ich habe gesehen, dass..."), which is about not asserting facts about someone you have
never met. This rule is about the *organisation's* published material, where the fix is the
opposite: drop the source, keep the fact, own it as understanding.

(that one is the deliberate opposite case: in a deliverable, figures DO need their sources; this
rule is about 1:1 messages only).
