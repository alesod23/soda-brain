Stylistic defaults for warm-intro outreach (LinkedIn DM, connect notes, cold email referencing a mutual contact):

**Opening — use "I'm writing to you on X's recommendation"**
- ✅ "I'm writing to you on Jörg Thomaier's recommendation..."
- ❌ "Jörg Thomaier suggested I reach out..."
- ❌ "X told me to get in touch..."

**Closing — use "expert opinion" not "view" or "perspective"**
- ✅ "I'd love to also hear your expert opinion from the pharma side."
- ✅ "I'd love your expert opinion from the telecom/SEP side, where the disclosure stakes look very different."
- ❌ "I'd love your view from..."
- ❌ "I'd love your perspective from..."

**Also: drop the contrastive tail when not needed** — "where the disclosure stakes look very different from pharma" → "where the disclosure stakes look very different." The reader doesn't need the comparison spelled out.

**Why:** User corrected all three drafts in the Jörg Thomaier referral batch (2026-05-22 — Lutze/Koerner/Mohsler) with these exact changes. "On X's recommendation" reads more formal/respectful for senior IP-world contacts; "expert opinion" signals you're treating them as the authority, not just one of many opinions. Confirmed pattern after the user dictated three near-identical edits across three drafts.

**How to apply:** Default to these on any warm-intro outreach — pharma execs, senior IP attorneys, professors, investors, anyone where formality + acknowledgement-of-expertise matters more than casualness. For peer/student/casual contacts the old phrasing is still fine. Related: [[reference_triage_v2_reply]], [[feedback_professor_email_style]].
